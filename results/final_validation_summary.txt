1. SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS ON HORIZON DEPENDENCE
Does tail dependence change with investment horizon?
- Evidence: Out-of-sample estimates show minimal change in tail dependence between short (D1, ~0.062) and long (D5, ~0.069) horizons. The difference is statistically indistinguishable from zero for most asset pairs.
- Conclusion: Tail dependence for this specific portfolio does not materially strengthen at longer investment horizons.

2. ABLATION MODEL PERFORMANCE (OUT-OF-SAMPLE)
What does ignoring multiscale tail dependence do to portfolio risk measurement?
- M1 (Heavy-Tail Marginal, no wavelets) VaR: -0.0298, ES: -0.0391. Breach rate: 1.19%.
- M3 (Full Multiscale Model) VaR: -0.0152, ES: -0.0176. Breach rate: 6.35%.
- Conclusion: The heavy-tail marginal model (M1) successfully calibrates to the 99% confidence level (1.19% breaches). The multiscale model (M3) severely underestimates risk, producing VaR forecasts that are roughly half of M1's and leading to excessive breaches (6.35%). Decomposing and reconstructing returns via wavelets and copulas degrades out-of-sample risk coverage.

3. ROBUSTNESS
- The findings are robust to variations in EVT threshold (90%), training window length (750 vs 1000 days), Monte Carlo simulation counts (1000), and random seeds. In all variations, M1 significantly outperforms M3 in backtest coverage.

======================================================================
EVIDENCE-BASED RISK MANAGER RECOMMENDATION
======================================================================

Action: Rely on the Heavy-Tail Marginal Model (M1) for production risk measurement and abandon the Multiscale Wavelet Vine Model (M3).

Why: The empirical validation strictly contradicts the hypothesis that a complex multiscale dependence structure improves risk forecasting for this portfolio. While the multiscale framework (M3) introduces significant complexity and computational cost, it fails out-of-sample, generating a 6.35% breach rate for a 99% VaR target. In contrast, the much simpler M1 model appropriately sizes tail risk with a 1.19% breach rate. Furthermore, the foundational assumption of the wavelet model—that tail dependence changes significantly with horizon—was not supported by the data. 

Risk managers should always favor parsimonious models that perform well out-of-sample over theoretically complex models that fail basic coverage tests.
